# SSA-44 Screener: Life-Changing Events

Form SSA-44 ("Medicare Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount – Life-Changing Event") asks SSA to use a more recent year's income (an estimate is allowed) instead of the 2-years-ago tax return. Confirm the current form and instructions at ssa.gov before every use.

## The qualifying events (ask about each one)

| # | Event | Screening question | Typical proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Marriage | "Did you get married in the last 2 years?" | Marriage certificate |
| 2 | Divorce / annulment | "Divorced or annulled since then?" | Decree |
| 3 | Death of spouse | "Did you lose your spouse?" | Death certificate |
| 4 | **Work stoppage** | "**Did you retire or stop working?**" | Employer letter, final pay stub, or signed statement |
| 5 | Work reduction | "Did you cut back your hours or move to part-time?" | Employer letter, pay stubs |
| 6 | Loss of income-producing property | "Lost rental or business property to a disaster, theft, or similar? (not normal market losses)" | Insurance claim, police report |
| 7 | Loss/reduction of employer pension | "Did a pension stop or get cut (plan termination, reorganization)?" | Pension plan letter |
| 8 | Employer settlement payment | "Did you get a settlement from an employer's closure, bankruptcy, or reorganization?" | Settlement documentation |

**Most common by far: #4, work stoppage (retirement).** Anyone who retired in the last ~2 years and has IRMAA is a likely candidate.

## Does NOT qualify (common misunderstandings)
- A one-time capital gain, home sale, or Roth conversion that inflated income.
- Required minimum distributions or voluntary IRA withdrawals.
- Stock-market losses.

These need **planning ahead** (see planning-levers.md), not an SSA-44.

## Wrong data instead? Use reconsideration, not SSA-44
If SSA used the wrong tax year, a return that was later amended, or simply incorrect data, the route is a **request for reconsideration (Form SSA-561)** or contacting SSA with the corrected return.

## Process (what to tell the client)
1. Client gets the IRMAA initial determination letter (most arrive in late fall for the next year; new enrollees get one when they enroll).
2. Gather: the letter, proof of the event, and an estimate of the current-year MAGI (or the actual return if filed).
3. File SSA-44 with SSA (mail, fax, local office, or appointment). Keep copies and the date filed.
4. If approved, SSA recalculates; any overpayment is refunded or credited. If denied, the letter explains appeal rights.
5. **Calendar a follow-up:** if the estimate was too low, SSA can adjust later. Ask for the actual return the next year.
